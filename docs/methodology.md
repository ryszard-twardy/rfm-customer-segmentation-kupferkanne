# Methodology

This project applies RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) segmentation to a 39-month synthetic dataset representing an Erlangen-based D2C e-commerce brand operating in nine European markets. The methodology is grounded in dimensional modelling, transparent statistical thresholds, and decisions documented through Architecture Decision Records.

## Approach

The end-to-end workflow follows the standard data engineering progression: audit raw data → clean and validate → explore distributions → transform into analytical structures → build presentation marts → expose through Power BI.

Each step is implemented as an idempotent SQL script (`CREATE OR REPLACE`) so that the pipeline can be re-run from any point without side effects. The nine numbered pipeline scripts in `sql/` execute in numerical order, with two standalone view scripts alongside; see [architecture.md](architecture.md) for the high-level flow and [ADR 0003](adr/0003-pipeline-order-eda-before-transform.md) for the ordering rationale.

## Data origin

The dataset is synthetic, generated by [synth-datagen](https://github.com/ryszard-twardy/synth-datagen) – a Python CLI tool built alongside this project to produce realistic e-commerce data with intentional quality issues: duplicate orders, cents-format inconsistencies, orphan keys, missing values, type drift across shards.

The cleaning pipeline (step 01) handles these issues end-to-end. Generating data this way (rather than using a pristine Kaggle dataset) creates a genuine cleaning workload while keeping reproducibility a property of the generator. See [ADR 0008](adr/0008-synthetic-data-with-realistic-quality-issues.md).

## Synthetic data – known limitation

The cohort retention view on Page 5 exhibits a pattern that, taken at face value, would read as a severe and worsening retention problem: month-1 retention falls from roughly 86% for the earliest (January 2023) cohorts to the 15–20% range for cohorts acquired in mid-2024. A decline of that magnitude in real data would be the headline finding of the entire analysis. It is not presented as one here, because it is an artifact of the data generator rather than a property of customer behaviour.

Before treating the gradient as an insight, it was tested against the mechanics of [synth-datagen](https://github.com/ryszard-twardy/synth-datagen). The generator allocates repeat orders from a monthly budget that stays approximately flat while the active customer base grows roughly threefold across the window. Per-capita reorder probability – and therefore each cohort's month-1 retention – falls mechanically as calendar time advances and the same budget is spread across more customers. The correlation between cohort month-1 retention and the budget-to-base ratio is strong (Pearson r = 0.879), consistent with a population-dilution mechanism rather than genuine decay.

The decisive evidence is a rebound that genuine attrition cannot produce: November-signup cohorts recover by roughly 20 points at month-1, because their second active month lands in the elevated December budget. Real retention decays monotonically within a cohort; a metric that climbs when the calendar budget rises is measuring the allocator, not the customer. Determinism was confirmed in passing – two runs under the same seed are byte-identical – which rules out random-number drift as the cause.

Two consequences follow. First, the heatmap is retained on the dashboard but framed as a data-quality observation, not a business recommendation; the retention metric itself is activity-based (a customer counts as retained in any month they reorder), which is non-monotonic by design and distinct from survival retention. Second, the fix – scaling the repeat-order budget to the eligible base – is tracked in the generator's own repository, and this project deliberately pins the pre-fix release so that the documented pipeline remains exactly reproducible.

## Quality audit (steps 00_0 and 01_1)

Two audits bookend the cleaning step. The pre-clean audit (00_0) runs 29 checks against raw shards for schema compliance, type drift, header-row contamination, referential integrity, and value-level issues. The post-clean audit (01_1) re-runs the same 29 checks against validated staging tables, allowing direct before/after comparison. Both audits write findings to dedicated audit tables for inspection in BigQuery. The pipeline does not abort on issues – it documents them. Cleaning rules are explicit and version-controlled.

## Exploratory analysis (step 02)

Eight EDA views run on cleaned staging data **before** any segmentation transformation. They answer the questions that inform RFM design:

- **`eda_order_value_distribution`** – percentile spread (P10–P99) guides Monetary thresholds.
- **`eda_order_value_outliers`** – surfaces transactions above P99 for plausibility review.
- **`eda_country_breakdown`** – market concentration and per-country AOV.
- **`eda_monthly_temporal_pattern`** – seasonality, growth trajectory, gap detection.
- **`eda_customer_frequency_distribution`** – validates that Frequency has enough variation for NTILE(5).
- **`eda_recency_distribution`** – confirms the choice of `MAX(order_date)` as recency anchor over `CURRENT_DATE()`.
- **`eda_pareto_concentration`** – revenue share by customer decile (the "80/20 test").
- **`eda_basket_composition`** – cross-validation of line-grain join against order-grain expectations.

EDA runs before RFM, not after, so segmentation decisions are evidence-based rather than assumed.

## RFM segmentation (step 03)

For each customer, three behavioural scores are computed on the order-grain fact `sales_curated`:

- **Recency**: days since most recent order, anchored on the dataset's `MAX(order_date)`.
- **Frequency**: total order count over the 39-month window.
- **Monetary**: total spend (`SUM(order_value)`).

Each dimension is bucketed 1–5 using `NTILE(5)` quintiles. The composite RFM score (range 3–15) is the sum of the three component scores. Six business-meaningful segments are derived from threshold bands on the composite:

| Composite score | Segment | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| 13–15 | Champions | Reward and cross-sell premium |
| 11–12 | Loyal Customers | Loyalty programme and early access |
| 9–10 | Potential Loyalists | Upsell and increase frequency |
| 7–8 | Recent Customers | Onboarding and second purchase push |
| 5–6 | At Risk | Win-back campaign and incentives |
| 3–4 | Hibernating | Deep discount or sunset |

The choice of `NTILE(5)` over alternatives (k-means clustering, single composite score) is documented in [ADR 0006](adr/0006-rfm-segmentation-with-ntile.md). The choice of `MAX(order_date)` as recency anchor – rather than `CURRENT_DATE()` – is covered in the same ADR, since using current date would inflate all recency values uniformly when the dataset ends in the past.

**Limitation – additive scoring.** Summing R+F+M into a single composite discards which dimension drives the total: a lapsed high-value customer (R=1, F=5, M=5) and a recently active mid-value one can land in the same band despite different lifecycle positions, and the segment names carry recency connotations the sum alone does not enforce. This is an accepted trade-off for a transparent, SQL-only scoring rule; a production variant would gate segment membership on the R score explicitly (the classic RFM-cell approach).

## Margin calculation

Profit margin is calculated as a **weighted** quantity: `SUM(profit) / SUM(revenue)` aggregated at the segment, country, or product grain. This contrasts with `AVG(margin_per_order)`, which double-weights small orders and produces misleading segment-level summaries. See [ADR 0007](adr/0007-two-tier-margin-calculation.md).

## DAX style conventions

All DAX measures follow the SQLBI short-line convention – the de-facto community standard popularised by Marco Russo and Alberto Ferrari ([sqlbi.com](https://www.sqlbi.com)). Concretely: spaces inside parentheses (`SUM ( table[column] )`), UPPER-case keywords, multi-line layout for any function taking more than one argument, and `VAR` / `RETURN` blocks preferred over nested inline expressions. Measures are run through [daxformatter.com](https://www.daxformatter.com/) before commit. The full catalogue lives in [measures.md](measures.md).

## Reactivation modelling

The Churn page (PBI Page 4) includes a What-If parameter allowing the user to simulate reactivation of cold customers at success rates from 0% to 50% in 5-point steps (default 10%); 5%–25% is the realistic planning band for win-back campaigns. The model projects incremental revenue under the assumption that the reactivated cohort behaves at the average of the next-warmer segment. This is **illustrative**, not predictive – no machine learning is applied, and the simplification is documented in the dashboard subtitle.

## Validation

In addition to the pre/post-clean audits, three cross-checks run during transformation:

1. **Grain parity** – order-grain revenue equals line-grain revenue aggregated to orders, asserted in the semantic model via the `[Grain Reconciliation]` measure (expected 0) – confirms the dual-grain join is consistent.
2. **Segment monotonicity** – the average Monetary score increases monotonically from Hibernating to Champions, confirming the composite score is well-behaved.
3. **Country coverage** – every country in `dim_customers` appears in the segmented output.

## Power BI consumption

The dashboard uses Import mode with the dual-grain semantic layer described in [data_model.md](data_model.md). Seven pages cover:

1. Executive Summary – KPIs and time-series.
2. Segment Deep Dive – segment behaviour and migration.
3. Product & Brand – line-grain product performance.
4. Churn Risk & What-If – reactivation modelling.
5. Customer Lifecycle Intelligence – RFM-space position, revenue concentration, cohort retention and segment migration.
6. Regional Analysis – country and city breakdown.
7. Customer Drillthrough – individual customer inspection.

All DAX measures are formatted to the SQLBI convention via [daxformatter.com](https://www.daxformatter.com/) and catalogued in [measures.md](measures.md).
